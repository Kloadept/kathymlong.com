The story

From Akron to the Front Range.

I was born in Akron, Ohio, and adopted at two weeks old into a very Italian family, where I grew up with an older sister ahead of me. Nobody in our family had gone to college, so I went first. I played soccer all through high school and kept playing at The University of Toledo, where I learned the thing every team sport eventually teaches you: show up, do your job, and cover for the person next to you. I'm five feet tall, and I have never once been short.

After college I moved to Chicago and started my career in telecom, selling secure networks and data center space to the financial sector, the infrastructure the money ran on. Chicago is also where I met my husband, and two weeks after our wedding we moved to Colorado. It was his dream, and it took me about a day to make it mine. We fell in love with the Boulder area and never left.

I kept going in telecom here, and a year later Jack was born, early and with far more going on than anyone expected. Jack is autistic, and he changed the shape of everything, starting with my job, because I stayed home. Somewhere in those early years I started a business selling and servicing cloth diapers, because Jack's skin hated disposables and I needed an outlet, and I discovered along the way that I love owning a business. Three more kids arrived, and the house filled up.

In between telecom jobs I went back to school for nursing and spent a year at a children's hospital, which gave me a front-row seat to kids on the worst day of their lives: severe disabilities, cancer, abuse, accidents nobody should ever see. I held burn patients through entire twelve-hour shifts because they were too terrified to sleep in a bed. I read books to kids waiting on transplants, and I helped toddlers who wanted no part of it get through ostomy changes. It was the best job I ever had, by a mile, and it could take your heart out on the same day. When my husband lost his job, I went back to tech because tech paid. Those memories are seared in.

Back in telecom I rose into leadership, then moved into absence management, where I supported employers and their attorneys on leave and benefits programs and learned to read the law closely while caring about the person on the other end of the policy. From there I jumped into startups and fell for the rush of it: product fires, support escalations, revenue operations, the chance to touch every part of a business before lunch. I built the Green-to-Growth Framework because I kept watching churn data get thrown away when it should have been read as growth intelligence. Eventually, running a startup as its head of operations made one thing clear to me: I was too itchy not to try it on my own.

Executive function in our house has always been a team sport, and for a long time I was most of the team. Over those years I watched the help that was on offer for Jack, and for brains like his, and it kept failing in the same way. It hovered, it nagged, and it tried to sand people down until they looked compliant. None of it protected him, and none of it knew how to get out of the way.

We don't make anyone normal. We make them unbreakable.

NixIt is what I built instead, with Jack as the first person I had in mind. It works over text message, because that's what people actually have in their hand, and it connects the individual, the people who love them, and the coach who works with them without turning any of them into a supervisor. It is armor, not compliance.

I hold a master's degree in organizational leadership and a bachelor's in communication and public relations, and I sit on the advisory board of Women in AI Colorado. I live in Broomfield, Colorado, just outside Boulder, where the air is thin and the view does most of the talking.

Long

Kathy M. Long is the founder and CEO of NixIt AI, an SMS-first support platform that helps neurodivergent people manage daily life by text message and connects them with their family circle and their coach. She started NixIt for her son Jack, who is autistic, and for a house full of brains that run a different OS, after three decades of operating experience convinced her that the help on offer hovered too much and protected too little.

Kathy's career began in telecom in Chicago, selling secure networks and data center infrastructure to the financial sector. In Colorado she stepped away to raise her first child, founded and ran a cloth diaper business, went back to school for nursing and spent a year at a children's hospital, then returned to telecom and rose into leadership before moving into absence management, supporting employers and their attorneys on leave and benefits programs. She spent the last stretch before NixIt in startups, running support, revenue operations, and eventually operations as a whole, and created the Green-to-Growth Framework, a method for turning churn data into growth intelligence.

Kathy was born in Akron, Ohio, was the first in her family to go to college, and played soccer at The University of Toledo before starting her career in Chicago. She now lives in Broomfield, Colorado, outside Boulder. She holds a master's degree in organizational leadership and a bachelor's degree in communication and public relations, and serves on the advisory board of Women in AI Colorado. She speaks on AI for neurodivergent lives, founding in disability tech, and running a company and a family of six at the same time.
